# Password guidance for people

## P01 · Prefer phishing-resistant authentication

Use passkeys or another phishing-resistant authentication method when a service offers one. Passwords are not phishing-resistant.

## P02 · Use a unique password for every account

Password reuse turns one service breach into a risk to other accounts.

## P03 · Use a password manager

Let a reputable password manager generate and store long, random, unique passwords. Protect the manager itself with strong authentication.

## P04 · Prefer length and unpredictability over symbol recipes

When you must create a password you can remember, use a long passphrase that is not based on personal facts or predictable substitutions.

## P05 · Spaces are allowed — but they are not magic

A space is a valid password character and can make passphrases easier to build. Security comes from the whole password: length, unpredictability and uniqueness, not from one character.

## P07 · Do not rotate passwords on a calendar

Change a password when there is evidence or reasonable suspicion of compromise, or when it was reused in another compromised service. Do not require arbitrary periodic changes.

## P10 · Protect recovery paths and respond quickly to compromise

Secure the primary email account and recovery methods. If a password may be compromised, change it, revoke old sessions where possible and review account recovery settings.
